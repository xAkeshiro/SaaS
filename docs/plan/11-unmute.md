# Unmute (working name): practice the conversation before it happens (13 Sep 2026)

*Talk to an AI so you can talk to people. Not a companion. A coach that exists to make you need it less.*

Working-name alternatives: Say It, Out Loud, Rehearsal. Check trademarks.

---

## 1. What the YC companies with the most users share

Looked at the YC companies that reached the largest user bases (Replit 50M users, Supabase 10M developers, Speak 15M downloads and $100M ARR, Whatnot, Kalshi) and at YC's own guidance. Eight things recur:

1. **A job people do daily or weekly, with value in the first five minutes.** Speak gets you speaking a language out loud in your first session; Supabase gives you a database in one click.
2. **Free to start, self-serve, no sales call.** Every one of them grew bottom-up.
3. **A platform wave to ride.** Supabase went from 1M to 4.5M developers in under a year because AI coding tools needed a backend; 40% of the latest YC batch builds on it.
4. **Distribution built into the product or the community**, not bought: open source and content loops (Supabase), creators bringing audiences (Whatnot), users bringing the people they practice with.
5. **Retention as the compass.** YC's growth advice starts with retention; Series (YC-adjacent, Yale, $5.1M pre-seed in April 2026) reports 82% day-30 retention and is on 750 campuses. Speak's users practice 5–10x more than on other apps.
6. **Founders building something they want themselves, that they can build, and that few others see as worth doing** (Paul Graham's three tests for the best ideas).
7. **A narrow wedge into a huge market**: Speak started with Korean learners of English; Whatnot with Funko Pops.
8. **A thesis loud enough for the press to repeat.** Cluely proved this works even when the product is thin; the lesson is to attach the loud thesis to a product that retains.

The batch data also says where not to look: consumer is 5% of YC W26, and the consumer winners are rare but enormous when the eight conditions line up.

## 3. The idea: Unmute

**The job.** Every person has a conversation they are dreading this week: the call to the doctor's office, the ask for a raise, the roommate talk, the first date, the career-fair pitch, the interview, the customer-service fight, the extension request to a professor, the toast, the breakup, saying no. Unmute lets you rehearse it out loud with an AI that plays the other person, realistically, and then tells you exactly what to do differently. Three minutes a day, like Speak, but for talking to humans in your own language.

**Why it will spread.**

- **The problem is universal and loudly documented.** 65% of Gen Z say calling a stranger makes them uncomfortable; 75% feel anxious about phone calls; only a third are comfortable making calls at all versus 67% of Boomers; 37% of adults 18–25 report significant anxiety, the highest of any adult group. "Gen Z can't talk to people" is a recurring press genre, and 57% of college students report loneliness.
- **The technology arrived this year.** Full-duplex, interruptible, emotionally responsive voice AI crossed the line in 2025–26 (Sesame's Maya and Miles, OpenAI's realtime models, ElevenLabs Agents at about $0.08 a minute). Practicing with a stiff chatbot was pointless; practicing with something that interrupts you, gets impatient, or warms up when you do is not.
- **The mechanic is proven at $100M ARR.** Speak's whole thesis is "get people to speak out loud as much as possible" and reward daily reps; it doubled every year for five years. Unmute is that thesis pointed at the conversations people actually fear, which is a bigger market than a second language.
- **It has a built-in reason to bring friends.** Practice rooms: your friend plays the recruiter while the AI coaches, or the AI plays the panel while your friends watch and rate. Dares: "call the pizza place and negotiate a discount" with a shareable clip. The before-and-after clip (your first call versus your tenth) is native TikTok content, and the phone-anxiety genre already has an audience.
- **It is the anti-companion.** Character.ai is under regulation and wrongful-death settlements; Friend is a symbol of backlash. A coach whose stated goal is to make you need it less is the story journalists want to write about voice AI right now.

**What it is, concretely.**

1. **Rehearse.** Pick a scenario from the library or describe your own ("my landlord won't return my deposit"). The AI plays the other side with a consistent personality, a voice, a mood, and difficulty settings from "kind" to "hostile". Full-duplex: it interrupts, sighs, goes quiet, pushes back.
2. **Debrief.** Instantly after: what worked, where you folded, filler words, pace, how long you took to get to the ask, a replay with highlights, and the two sentences to try next time. A confidence trend over weeks. The AI remembers your patterns ("you apologize before every ask").
3. **The daily rep.** Three minutes, streaks, a scenario the AI picks from what you have coming up (it can read your calendar for "interview Thursday" if you let it).
4. **Real-mode.** Before a real call: a 60-second warmup and a cue card. After: a debrief from your own notes. Unmute never listens to real calls; iOS and Android do not allow it, and nobody would trust it if they did.
5. **Together.** Practice rooms with friends, dares, shareable clips (voice-changed if you want), and campus challenges in interview season.
6. **Guardrails.** It is a coach, not therapy: crisis language routes to real resources; no romantic roleplay; every persona is clearly synthetic.

**Business model.** Free: one rep a day. Plus at $14.99 a month or $99 a year, Speak's price band: unlimited reps, custom scenarios, real-mode, trends, practice rooms. Teams: universities' career centers (interview season is a budget line), companies (customer-facing and manager training; Yoodli proves enterprise demand), and clinicians who run structured social-anxiety practice with clients.

**Unit economics.** A three-minute rep costs $0.10–0.25 in voice inference today and is falling; a daily free user costs $3–7 a month, so the free tier stays at one rep and the hard paywall comes after the first debrief, where consumer AI apps convert best (10.7% versus 2.1% for soft paywalls).

## 5. Risks

- **ChatGPT adds a practice mode.** Speak survived ChatGPT by being structured, scored, and habitual. Same playbook: the debrief, the memory of your patterns, the curriculum and the rooms are the product, not the voice.
- **Voice cost.** Falling fast; the free tier is capped; the paid tier is priced above cost from day one.
- **Cringe.** Talking to your phone out loud in a dorm is awkward. Speak's 15M downloads say people do it; headphones, a "text-first then voice" on-ramp, and private rooms lower the bar.
- **Safety.** Vulnerable users will bring real fear to it. Clinical advisors, crisis routing, no romantic scenarios, clear synthetic disclosure.
- **Retention.** Skill apps retain when there is a next real conversation to prepare for; the calendar hook and the daily rep exist for that reason.
