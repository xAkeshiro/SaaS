# Facedown vs Asterisk: a product that spreads inside communities without events (13 Sep 2026)

**What changed from the last comparison.** Presence was built on your contacts and on events, which was not the brief. The brief is: something anyone can use, that spreads inside communities like a campus because people bring their friends, and that does not depend on a specific setting like an event. Facedown is that product. It works alone on day one, gets better with every friend who joins, and a "community" can be a dorm, a class, a company, a team, a church group or a gym, none of which requires an event.

Both earlier comparison documents (06 and 07) are superseded by this one.

---

## 1. Facedown (working name): the phone-down network

**The unit of value is a verified facedown hour.** You tap Facedown, put the phone down, and the app verifies the phone stayed locked using the operating system's lock state (not Apple's Screen Time API, which broke for most of iOS 26 and which forbids exporting any usage data to a third party, so no third-party screen-time leaderboard can ever exist on iOS). Facedown hours are the only number in the app, and they are always positive: hours you were present, never hours you wasted. Opal's own users say leaderboards of phone habits embarrass them; Facedown never shows a bad number.

**Single-player, day one.** A session, a streak, a widget, a weekly recap. This is the Focus Friend slot (a cute timer went to number one on the US App Store in August 2025 and became Google Play's App of the Year on creator distribution alone), which proves the demand and the price band (~$100k a month at a few dollars).

**Together.** Start a session with the people at the table: everyone's phone locks, the group session is verified, the first phone picked up ends its owner's streak (the phone-stack game, which is now default behavior at Gen Z dinners, made real). Or remote: friends see "Maya is facedown, 42 min" and join.

**Rooms, the community layer.** Any community gets a room: "UCLA: 312 people facedown right now, 12,480 hours this week." Join a room to be counted; rooms for a dorm, a class, a startup, a hospital shift, a writers' group. This is what the million-member Study Together Discord does in a clunky way, made mobile-native, community-scoped and verified. Rooms spread the product community by community without anyone organizing anything.

**Pacts.** Recurring group commitments, verified: facedown dinners, no phone after 11, a Sunday two-hour block. A pact wall shows who kept it. Optional stakes to charity later.

**Not a blocker.** Blocking is an optional add-on for people who want it; the core never depends on Apple's fragile shield API.

**Business model.** Free core. Plus at $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year: custom pacts, unlimited rooms, insights, widgets and themes. Later, Facedown for teams and campuses at $1–2 per member per year through wellness budgets, and sponsors for room challenges if wanted. Consumer AI and habit apps monetize best with a hard paywall after the first win and annual plans.

**Why now.** Phone-stacking is a norm, not a game; Gen Z dumb-phone and "intentionally disconnect" behavior is at 63%; 47% of under-30s are actively cutting screen time; phone-free events grew 567% in a year; NYU and other universities run device-free programs; Focus Friend showed a focus app can be the most-downloaded app in America.

**What exists, honestly.** This is not empty space. Opal (1M+ daily users, two-thirds students, $99 a year) has leaderboards and a "Buddies" feature that texts a friend when you slip. FocusDown flips face-down to start, has a mascot and friend challenges, and is tiny (12,481 flips in a week). LOCK IN is a blocker with friend challenges. Forest and Flora plant trees together. Focusmate ($10 a month) and Flow Club ($40 a month) sell scheduled body-doubling. Study Together has a million Discord members. Nobody has the community room layer, the phone-stack verification or the positive-only design, and no social focus app has broken out, but the category has many small entrants and a graveyard.

## 2. Head to head

| Criterion | Facedown | Asterisk |
|---|---|---|
| Value to the first user, alone | Medium: a verified session, a streak and a widget work with zero friends (the Focus Friend proof) | High: one lease review or one caught auto-renewal pays off alone |
| How it spreads inside a community | Strong: you invite the people at the table, rooms count everyone at your school or company, pacts pull in friends. No events, no organizer | Moderate: roommate groups for shared leases and subscriptions, a per-school landlord index, a school savings leaderboard. Mostly individual utility |
| Everyone can relate | Yes: 53% of Americans want to cut phone use | Yes: everyone signs things |
| Willingness to pay | Low: students near zero, others $30 a year; Opal proves ~$10M ARR is reachable at the top of the category | Medium to high: $40 a year for adults, plus credit unions and an agent API |
| Scale of the business | Consumer social: hits-driven; a good outcome is Opal-sized ($10–20M ARR), a great one is a cultural default | Consumer utility with a B2B2C channel; 2% of US households at $40 is ~$100M ARR |
| White space | Thin: many small entrants (FocusDown, LOCK IN), Opal's social features, no breakout. Differentiators are rooms, verification and positive-only design | Real: every current tool is upload-after-the-fact; Rocket Money is after-the-charge |
| Virality and content | Strongest: phone stacks are content, room counts are bragging rights, streaks are shareable | Good: "caught a trap" cards and the "read the fine print" format |
| Why now | Strong: phone-stack norm, dumb-phone trend, Focus Friend | Strongest: click-to-cancel vacated, state laws spreading, Honey trust collapse, agents accepting terms |
| Platform risk | Moderate: lock-state verification is stable; Apple could add social features to Screen Time | Moderate: Apple could add renewal warnings to Safari |
| Build risk for me | Medium: native app with lock-state detection, a social backend, rooms, moderation | Low to medium: extension, web, mobile share sheet |
| Retention risk | High: attention apps habituate in weeks; rooms and pacts are the answer, unproven | Medium: the vault and alerts give a reason to keep it installed |
| After college | Keeps working: rooms for companies and teams | Keeps working: contracts get bigger |
| Regulatory or trust risk | Low | Low if there is no affiliate revenue and no "AI lawyer" language |

Asterisk wins on business fundamentals (white space, payer, build risk). Facedown wins on the thing you asked for (spread inside communities) and on virality. Two points is not a verdict; it is a choice about what kind of company you want.

## 3. The choice, plainly

- **Pick Facedown if** the goal for the first year is users and cultural momentum in communities you can reach, you accept that revenue lags (consumer social apps monetize late and unevenly), and you want the product people talk about at dinner. The unfair advantage is that you know communities that will seed rooms; that is a launch tactic, not the product.
- **Pick Asterisk if** the goal for the first year is a business with paying users by month three, a clear path to partners, and less dependence on hits. Its campus launch still uses your communities (lease clinics, roommate groups, the school index), it just does not need them.

**The cheapest test before building either:** a one-page site for each with a single call to action. Facedown: "Create a room for your community" with a waitlist that asks for the community name. Asterisk: "Check a lease or a subscription for traps" with an upload box that returns one sample card. Send both to the same 200 people in your communities. Whichever gets more sign-ups and more forwards in a week is the one to build first. The other stays on the shelf as the second product.

## 4. If you pick Facedown: the first 90 days

| Weeks | Milestone | Success measure |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Mobile app (Expo): lock-state sessions, streaks, widget, friend status, the in-person stack session | Verified sessions work on iPhone and Android; a five-person stack session verifies correctly |
| 3–6 | Rooms (create, join, live count, weekly hours), pacts v1, shareable recap cards | 10 rooms seeded in your communities; 40% of room members active in a week |
| 6–13 | Plus tier with a hard paywall after the first week of sessions; creator and community seeding; Android widgets | Week-4 retention above 30% (attention apps usually fail this); 3–5% paid conversion |

**Kill or scale:** if week-4 retention is under 25% with rooms live, the mechanic does not beat habituation and Asterisk becomes the product. If rooms hold above 30% and stack sessions are a weekly habit for a third of users, scale to companies and teams.
