# Market research: where is the white space for a new SaaS? (12 Sep 2026)

**Method.** Four parallel research passes (startup landscape, phone/attention market, student and everyday pain points, startup tooling and school procurement) plus targeted checks, about 200 web searches in total. The sandbox's egress proxy blocked most direct page fetches, so figures come from search-result extracts of the cited pages. Treat every number as "reported, spot-check before using externally." Items marked *(unverified)* are vendor-sourced, small-sample, or could not be traced to a primary source.

## 2. In 2025–2026 the problem became policy

**K-12 phone bans.** At least 38 states plus DC require districts to restrict phones and 28 states are bell-to-bell ([Newsweek/EdWeek tracker, 2026](https://www.newsweek.com/map-shows-us-states-with-school-phone-bans-in-2026-11335155)); 22 laws passed in 2025 alone ([CivicIQ](https://civiciq.com/blog/26-states-now-require-school-cell-phone-bans-2026-k-12-policy-tracker-implementation-guide)); 39 states and 106 countries by August 2026 ([US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/national-news/articles/2026-08-10/39-u-s-states-and-106-countries-now-prohibit-phones-in-class)). California's AB 3216 required every district to have a policy by 1 Jul 2026. South Carolina ties state classroom funding to a phone-free policy ([Free to Focus SC](https://ed.sc.gov/initiatives/free-to-focus/)).

**New in 2026: limits on school-issued screens.** Six states enacted classroom screen-time laws in 2026 ([MultiState, Apr 2026](https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/4/8/elementary-school-screen-time-limits-gain-momentum-in-2026); [Stateline, Jul 2026](https://stateline.org/2026/07/23/states-move-to-limit-classroom-screen-time/)). Iowa caps digital instruction at 60 minutes a day for K-5. Utah HB 273 requires model policies with age-based limits by 1 Dec 2026. Tennessee requires every K-5 district to adopt a device policy. Maine commissioned a study of instructional time on school devices. Districts will have to measure and report per-student screen minutes, and no incumbent (GoGuardian, Lightspeed, Securly) is purpose-built for that.

**What educators actually say.** The Phones in Focus survey (Duckworth, Allcott, Gentzkow, Dee) has 68k educator responses covering ~17% of US public schools: bell-to-bell share rose from 60% to 74% in one year; stricter policies mean less in-class use and happier teachers; pouches or lockers beat "keep it in your backpack." Its stated premise: **"schools don't know how well policies are working"** ([Ford School](https://fordschool.umich.edu/news/2026/first-its-kind-national-educator-survey-reveals-both-promise-and-peril-school-cell-phone), [After Babel](https://www.afterbabel.com/p/school-policy-survey-duckworth)). A July 2026 New Hampshire survey: ~80% of teachers say the ban works, but 36–41% say it is not consistently enforced ([InDepthNH](https://indepthnh.org/2026/07/29/school-cellphone-survey-indicates-ban-is-effective/)). Teachers' top complaint is enforcement dumped on them without clear discipline steps.

**What schools buy.** Yondr pouches at $25–30 per student, contracts from $5k to over $5M (LAUSD), Cincinnati $500k ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/yondr-pouches-school-cell-phone-ban/), [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/schools-spend-millions-yondr-pouches-store-cellphones-rcna230454)); 15–20% annual replacement and $11 fishing-magnet hacks *(unverified)*. LockedIn, built by two LA high-schoolers, sells a "software Yondr" for K-12 at $5–20 per student with geofenced OS-level locking and an admin dashboard; student complaints include location tracking, battery drain, and being unable to unlock when leaving early ([LockedIn](https://www.lockedinapp.co/), [Milken Roar](https://milkenroar.com/52631/news/are-students-more-locked-in-with-the-lockedin-app/)).

**Colleges have no product at all.** NYU launched NYU IRL in early 2026, a university-wide device-free initiative: a lounge with phone lockers (~60 students a day), device-free dinners with 300 attendees, 2,000+ participants, faculty "device optimization" resources, across New York, Abu Dhabi and Shanghai ([NYU](https://www.nyu.edu/about/university-initiatives/nyu-irl.html), [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/03/02/nyu-launches-device-free-spaces-students), [US News](https://www.usnews.com/education/u-s-news-higher-ground/articles/2026-07-09/mental-health-digital-detox-nyu)). Loyola Maryland runs a digital-detox course. Faculty-level classroom bans are spreading ([Inside Higher Ed, Jan 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/opinion/views/2026/01/23/classroom-attention-sanctuary-opinion)). Wellness centers publish digital-wellness pages but buy telehealth (TimelyCare), not attention tools.

**The mechanism is validated.** An August 2025 study in the Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization (Aksoy, RPI, with Pitt and UT Austin) found college students who used Pocket Points, which locked the phone during class for redeemable points, reported fewer distractions, better attendance, higher academic satisfaction and suggestive grade gains. The authors frame voluntary incentives as a "commitment device" that beats blanket bans for adult students ([RPI](https://news.rpi.edu/2025/08/07/rpi-study-suggests-incentives-are-valuable-alternative-school-cell-phone-bans), [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marybethgasman/2025/09/28/how-incentives-not-bans-curb-college-phone-distractions/)).

## 3. What has been tried, and why it died

| Product | Model | Outcome | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pocket Points (2014–2024) | Lock phone in class, earn local-merchant coupons; 40% of Penn State in weeks; 150+ campuses; $600k seed | Ratings fell to ~2/5, churn, removed from Google Play Feb 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_Points), [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/pocket-points-student-rewards/com.pocketpoints.pocketpoints)) | Distribution was never the problem. Coupon economics and no institutional payer were. |
| Hold (Norway 2016) | Same model, brand coupons; 40% of Norwegian HE students; $1.1M raise, moved to London | 2025–26 status unverified | Same monetization trap. |
| Moment (2014–2021) | Screen-time measurement | Apple built Screen Time in 2018; downloads fell; shut down ([kevinholesh.com](https://kevinholesh.com/moment)) | Measurement alone is a platform feature. |
| Siempo (2016–2020) | Humane Android launcher; $1.1M raised, zero revenue | Dissolved ([Failory](https://www.failory.com/interview/siempo)) | Mission without a payer dies. |
| Opal (live) | Consumer blocker, $99.99/yr; $10M ARR with 11 staff in 2024, ~$17M est. 2025 *(unverified)* | Cut paid conversion from 20% to 9% for virality; sessions cancellable unless paid; rides a buggy Apple API | Students dominate usage and cannot pay. |
| Brick, Unpluq (live) | NFC hardware key + app; Brick >60k units, ~$2M/yr *(unverified)* | "Status symbol" niche ([Fortune, Feb 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/02/13/analog-gen-z-phone-addiction-bloom-brick-app-blockers-dumb-phones-social-media/)) | Hardware sells; software alone habituates. |
| one sec, ScreenZen, Clearspace, Jomo, Roots | Friction interstitials, $5–20/yr | "Works until it doesn't"; habituation in weeks; iOS 26 regressions broke shields | Willpower-based friction decays. |
| Microsoft Viva Goals | Enterprise wellbeing add-on | Retired 31 Dec 2025 | Even Microsoft prunes wellbeing tools without ROI. |

Cross-cutting lessons from the graveyard: (1) any iOS-native blocker is one Apple release away from breakage or sherlocking, so the value must live outside the shield; (2) the durable money is institutional (Yondr's $5M contracts vs. Brick's ~$2M/yr total); (3) accountability with other people and institution-defined context (class time, school hours) are the only levers that do not depend on willpower.

## 4. Technical reality

- **iOS.** FamilyControls, ManagedSettings and DeviceActivity give third parties a controlled subset: opaque app tokens, no network in extensions, no passcode protection (a user can revoke in Settings), entitlement approval per bundle ID (days to weeks). iOS 26 shipped regressions (thresholds never fire, corrupted usage data) that went ~10 months before a partial fix in 26.5 beta ([Apple dev forum](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/819997), [riedel.wtf](https://riedel.wtf/state-of-the-screen-time-api-2024/)). WWDC26 added school-hour schedules to Screen Time for child accounts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/apple-puts-parents-back-in-control-of-kids-iphone-use/)), which sherlocks parent-facing schedule apps but not adult, institution-facing products.
- **A native app can reliably detect that the phone is locked** (iOS protected-data notifications, Android screen-off broadcasts) without touching the Screen Time API. That is enough to verify "phone stayed locked for 50 minutes," which is the Pocket Points mechanism.
- **Android.** UsageStatsManager covers monitoring; AccessibilityService blocking is policy-restricted and Advanced Protection Mode (Feb 2026) blocks it; overlay plus UsageStats polling is the durable path.
- **Web/PWA.** Cannot shield apps. Can do everything else: accountability, schedules, commitments, group sessions, dashboards, institutional admin, desktop blocking via extension. iOS 26 runs PWAs in default web-app mode with push ([MagicBell](https://www.magicbell.com/blog/pwa-ios-limitations-safari-support-complete-guide)).
- **Fastest mobile path:** Expo with a custom dev client and config plugins; file the Family Controls request on day one only if shielding becomes a paid add-on; ship web for everything that is not lock detection or push.

## 5. Where venture attention is, and is not

YC's six Requests for Startups from W25 to F26 contain ~60 asks; only four touch consumers' everyday lives (a secure AI app store, personalized kids' video, AI tutoring for young children, AI for the aging population). The rest is B2B, infra, defense, energy, space and hard tech ([Inc, F26](https://www.inc.com/lucia-auerbach/y-combinator-requests-for-startups-fall-2026/91379840)). Consumer was 5.5% of the S26 batch (Demo Day 10 Sep 2026); industrials rose to 23% ([YC Insight](https://ycinsight.com/yc-s26)). Agent infrastructure is "now a category." Teacher AI copilots are crowded (MagicSchool 6M educators, Brisk, SchoolAI, Subject $28M, Gizmo $22M).

Edtech venture funding fell to $1.0B in 1H 2026 (-25.7%); K-12 startup funding is down 82% from peak; 645 new edtech companies launched in 2025 vs ~10,500 in 2020 ([HolonIQ](https://www.holoniq.com/notes/1b-in-edtech-venture-capital-to-date-funding-falls-short-of-last-years-midpoint-asia-mena-buck-the-trend), [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2026/edtech-funding-collapse-k12-startups-ai-workforce/)). What did get funded is K-12 *operations* (Minga $65M for behavior, tardies, hall passes and digital ID, Mar 2026; Zum $100M). Reach Capital closed a $265M fund on 18 Aug 2026.

Under-served areas named by investors or evidence: early-childhood tutoring at consumer scale; family caregiving; household admin (75% say household info is not organized, per Quicken 2026); multiplayer AI; "corner-shop" SMB compliance; and, per Lightspeed, a coming "secondary boom in classic consumer categories" as AI hands back time.

## 6. Student pain points, ranked by evidence

1. **Mental health is the #1 stop-out driver.** 1 in 3 students considered leaving; emotional stress 54%, mental health 43%, cost 31% (Gallup–Lumina, n=14,032). Counseling waitlists of 6–8 weeks *(unverified)*.
2. **Money and basic needs.** 42% cite finances as top barrier (Inside Higher Ed 2025); 59% food or housing insecure (Hope Center); 20% missed class for lack of transport (Trellis, n≈65,000).
3. **Course materials.** 70% skipped materials due to cost ([Student PIRGs, Feb 2026](https://studentpirgs.org/2026/02/10/fixing-the-broken-textbooks-market-fourth-edition/)).
4. **Registration.** 85% had trouble registering for a needed course ([US News, Mar 2026](https://www.usnews.com/education/getting-in/articles/2026-03-18/college-class-education-graduation-student)).
5. **Advising.** Under 50% aware of advising, 29% use it; caseloads rising ([Tyton 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/08/14/3133249/0/en/Tyton-Partners-Releases-Driving-Toward-a-Degree-2025-Career-Readiness-and-Financial-Pressures-at-the-Forefront-of-Student-Support.html)).
6. **AI-detection anxiety.** 94% of UK undergrads use genAI on assessed work and fear wrongful accusation; Vanderbilt turned off Turnitin's detector; detectors hit 61% false-positive rates on non-native English ([HEPI, Aug 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/2026/08/27/ai-detectors-and-the-fairness-gap-read-the-evidence-before-you-trust-the-score/)). Tyton "Time for Class 2026": over half of students, faculty and admins use AI weekly.
7. **Careers.** 15% fewer entry-level postings; 61% of the class of 2026 pessimistic (Handshake).
8. **Loneliness.** 57% lonely, 12% always ([Inside Higher Ed, Feb 2026](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/health-wellness/2026/02/11/student-loneliness-new-normal)).
9. **Deadline fragmentation** across LMS, email, Discord, GroupMe (moderate evidence). The April 2026 Canvas breach exposed ~275M records across ~8,800 institutions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Canvas_data_breach)), which makes "minimal data" a selling point for any new campus vendor.
10. **Group-project free-riding.** 80% frustrated.

Everyday adult pains with the strongest evidence: insurance denials (85% never appeal; 69% do not know they can, [KFF, Jan 2026](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/consumer-survey-highlights-problems-with-denied-health-insurance-claims/)); family caregiving (63M caregivers, AARP 2025); workplace interruptions every 2 minutes (Microsoft WTI 2025); "workslop" costing ~2 hours per item to fix (BetterUp/Stanford 2025). Life-admin assistants are a graveyard: Milo (YC/OpenAI-backed) shut down Jan 2026, Yohana (Panasonic) wound down Jan 2025.

## 7. How schools and universities buy

- **K-12:** 6–18 month cycles; fiscal year 1 Jul; budget planning Oct–Mar; RFPs Jan–Apr; informal-quote thresholds $5k–$50k; districts spend ~$621 per student per year on tech. Bottom-up teacher freemium that "bubbles up" outperforms top-down in most categories; "superintendents approve purchases, they do not discover them."
- **Higher ed:** 3 weeks (department card) to 18 months (state RFP). Per-student-per-year is the standard model; ~10% discount over 100 seats, 20% over 500. The bottom-up playbook is proven: UIC found ~1,100 students individually paying ~$77k/yr for Grammarly and bought a site license. Cal State bought ChatGPT Edu for 460k students; Claude for Education has full-campus deals at Northeastern, LSE, Champlain.
- **Student-success platforms** are expensive: one EAB Navigate contract was $278,000 a year. Student enrollment-management software is a $3.48B market in 2026.
- **Compliance checklist:** SDPC National Data Privacy Agreement v2 (vendors who cannot sign are "locked out"), FERPA, COPPA (amended rule in force 22 Apr 2026, separate consent for third-party disclosure including AI training), state laws (NY Ed Law 2-d, CA SOPIPA), LTI 1.3 for LMS integration, Clever/ClassLink rostering, SSO via Google Workspace or Microsoft Entra, VPAT/HECVAT, WCAG 2.1 AA (ADA Title II deadline Apr 2027), SOC 2 expected.
- **Cooperative purchasing** shortcuts RFPs: Sourcewell, OMNIA, NASPO ValuePoint for K-12; E&I for higher ed.
- **Campus ambassador programs** are standard (Notion, Perplexity, Claude, Figma), but student-subsidy programs get pulled for fraud (Cursor's free student Pro closed 25 Jun 2026), so verification matters.
